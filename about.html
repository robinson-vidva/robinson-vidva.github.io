What this site is for

Robinson Vidva is a computational biologist working across neurodevelopment, immuno-oncology, and drug discovery. The work centers on integrating transcriptomics, metabolomics, and genomics data to understand disease mechanisms and evaluate treatment options. Recent projects are in pediatric neurodevelopment at Children's National Hospital, Washington DC.

This site keeps that work in one place where other people can use it: the tools run in the browser, the research page lists every paper, presentation and patent, the projects page tells what each project produced, and the articles explain the methods. Collaborations, tool feedback, consulting, and research questions are welcome through the contact page.

Methods: multi-omics integration, NGS & variant analysis, machine learning, and systems-biology modeling.

Selected work

Neurodevelopmental data analysis

Multi-omics analysis of preterm-birth effects on brain development, with behavioral phenotyping in mouse models using DeepLabCut and B-SOiD.

Immuno-oncology & precision medicine

Patient-specific drug-response models built from cancer genomic data, trained and benchmarked on more than 500 cancer cell lines, plus multi-cell models of tumor and immune signaling.

Predictive disease modeling

Pathway models for autoimmune disease and cancer, checked against mouse, cell-line, and patient data. The work led to two patented drug combinations (three grants, US and European) and three published applications.

Open-source scientific tools

MyVivarium, an open-source platform for managing research animal colonies (first author, CSBJ 2025), plus the browser-based tools on the Tools & Demos page.
