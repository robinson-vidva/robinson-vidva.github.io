MyVivarium: Lab Animal Colony Management

An open-source platform for managing research animal colonies

Tools January 25, 2025 2 min read — views

Many labs still run their animal colonies on paper cage cards and spreadsheets. Commercial colony software exists, but it is expensive for a single lab, and the open-source options I looked at were hard to install and harder to use. MyVivarium is my attempt at something a small lab can afford and actually keep running.

It started in 2021 as a tool for one laboratory, the Sathyanesan lab at the University of Dayton, where it is still in use. I designed and built it, and led the paper describing it, written with colleagues from several labs and published in the Computational and Structural Biotechnology Journal in 2025. It now runs in three laboratories that together manage more than 200 animals.

What it does at the rack

Every cage gets a printed card with a QR code. Scanning it with a phone opens that cage's record, so a change made during a cage check is entered once, at the rack, instead of being copied from paper later. That single change removes most transcription errors.

Behind the cards is a central database of animals, breeding cages, and holding cages. It sends task reminders, tracks strains with their Research Resource Identifiers (RRIDs), and limits who can see or edit what through user roles.

Sensors in the room

Low-cost sensors in the housing room record temperature, humidity, light, and activity continuously and send them to the same system. When breeding drops or animals behave oddly, the room's conditions for the preceding days are already on record next to the cage data.

Running it

MyVivarium is a PHP and MySQL web application with a JavaScript front end. It runs on ordinary cloud hosting for about $6 a month, and a standard installation takes a few hours with the scripts and guides in the repository. Each facility runs its own copy, so its data never sits on anyone else's server. Connections are encrypted, passwords are stored hashed, and access is role-based. The code is released under the LGPL, and bugs and feature requests go through GitHub.

Version 2

The second version is packaged with Docker, so a lab can bring it up without configuring a server by hand, and it ships with a Playwright test suite that exercises the main workflows in a real browser. It is in staged deployment at the University of Dayton in 2026.

You can try it on the live demo or read the source at github.com/myvivarium.
